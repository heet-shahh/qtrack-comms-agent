Patient Communication Agent

2026-07-21 PRD · draft for review LCS + IPN Companion: Technical design →

This document describes what we are building and why. It is the product requirements, not the design. How it is built (architecture, the engine, the voice integration) lives in the companion technical document.

01 Problem

Blended follow-up adherence across lung screening (LCS) and incidental pulmonary nodules (IPN) at a mid-size health system sits around 38 percent, against an achievable target closer to 65 percent. The gap is not that the messages are poorly written. It is that no system today reliably does three things:

  • Turn a clinical or scheduling event into a tracked action that cannot silently die in an inbox.
  • Decide who needs to be contacted, how urgently, and whether a human is required.
  • Make sure the resulting effort, and its outcome, is recorded somewhere durable rather than in a navigator's memory or a spreadsheet.

Every missed follow-up is both a clinical-safety risk (a nodule that goes unwatched) and lost revenue (a billable scan that never happens). Closing the loop is the whole job.

02 Goals & non-goals

Goals

  • Every relevant trigger becomes a tracked Patient Communication Task with a goal and an owner.
  • Low-risk, logistical outreach runs automatically, with no human in the loop.
  • Conversations that need clinical judgment are navigator-led, with the agent doing the prep and the documentation.
  • An automated interaction that goes off-script hands off to a human gracefully, never guessing at a clinical answer.
  • Every task and outcome is durable and searchable, per patient, across programs.
  • Which staff role handles what, and how each workflow behaves, is configurable, not hardcoded.

Non-goals (this phase)

  • Extracting risk from free-text radiology reports. Classification uses structured fields already available.
  • Unsupervised AI conducting a clinical conversation. Clinical calls stay navigator-led.
  • Cross-program enrollment linking, and insurance or coverage-driven triggers.

03 Who it is for

UserWhat they need
Navigator (nurse / care coordinator)A short, prioritized list of who to call and why, the context to make the call well, and a fast way to log what happened. No system jargon.
Program admin / opsTo configure how each workflow behaves and to see what the automation is doing, without engineering help.
Patient (the recipient)Clear, brief, respectful outreach; an easy opt-out; and a real person the moment they have a question that matters.

04 Product principles

  • Channel-agnostic. The same workflow should work over text, voice, or whatever comes next. The channel is a delivery detail, not the product.
  • Goal-directed. Every outreach has one clear objective and knows whether it has been met.
  • Never guess at clinical questions. When a patient asks something that needs a person, the system acknowledges it and hands off, by name and with a deadline.
  • Everything is logged. Whether the outcome was good or bad, the record exists. This is the audit trail that makes the safety and liability story real.
  • Configurable, not hardcoded. Goals, retries, escalation rules, and next steps are settings, not code changes.

05 The two experiences

The people who do the clinical work are not the people who configure the system, so the product has two distinct experiences behind one login, gated by role.

Navigator (clinical)

The requirement is that this stay simple. A navigator must be able to:

  • See a prioritized worklist of patients who need a call, each described in one plain sentence (who, and why now).
  • Open a case and immediately see why it is here, the patient's own words if they asked something, and short talking points.
  • Log the call in one structured form, in plain language.
  • Never be shown internal states, workflow names, or configuration.

Requirement A task cannot be marked done until the outcome is captured. The record is the point; a closed task with no outcome is not allowed.

Admin (workflow management)

This is where the complexity is allowed to live. An admin must be able to:

  • Configure each workflow: its trigger, its goal, how many times to retry and how, which words escalate a case to a human, and what runs next on success.
  • See the whole task queue and the full event log.
  • See what the automation did for a patient or a panel, including tasks that never needed a human, so the automatic track is never a black box.

06 Workflow catalog

Five workflows in this phase. Four are automatic (simple yes / no / scheduling exchanges); one is assisted (an open clinical conversation).

The rule What decides automatic vs assisted is not how serious the case is. It is whether the useful reply can be anticipated. "Does Tuesday work" has a fixed set of answers, so it is automatic. "Is this serious" cannot be scripted, so it is always assisted, even when the underlying risk is routine.

07 The signature flow: a patient goes off-script

This is the behaviour the whole product is judged on, so it is a requirement, not a nice-to-have. When an automated message gets a reply it was not built for, the experience must follow these steps every time.

→ → → →
1 · The automated message

A patient gets a short, single-question reminder with an easy way to reply or opt out. Most of the time this resolves on its own, and no human is ever involved.

2 · A reply it was not built for

Instead of picking an option, the patient asks something clinical, or shows worry: "why do I even need this?", "no, I can't afford it." The system must recognize this is outside what it can handle.

3 · Acknowledge, do not answer

The system must not attempt a medical answer. It acknowledges the question warmly and makes clear a person will help.

4 · Name a person and a time

"I'll have Sarah, your care coordinator, call you back today." A named person and a same-day promise, plus a confirmation of anything already resolved (like the appointment) so nothing feels dropped.

5 · It reaches the right navigator

The case appears on the navigator's worklist with the patient's actual question visible and a same-day deadline attached. An acknowledged-but-unanswered question is a worse outcome than the original no-show, so it cannot sit.

09 Scope & phasing

In scope now (V1)

  • The five workflows above, four automatic and one assisted.
  • The two experiences: navigator worklist and admin workflow configuration.
  • The off-script handoff, a per-patient history, and a full event log.
  • At least one patient-facing channel and the navigator worklist itself.

Later

  • Voice as a delivery channel for the automatic workflows, and outbound calls to real phones (which brings in phone numbers and call-recording consent).
  • Additional workflows and channels, and self-service configuration for non-engineering staff.
  • Automated recording, transcription, and AI-drafted (navigator-confirmed) call summaries for the assisted workflow.

Note: a working prototype already demonstrates the two experiences, the five workflows over text, and a real voice call. See the technical document for what is actually built.

10 Open questions

  • Escalation sensitivity. Too loose and everything escalates, defeating the point; too tight and real concerns get missed. This probably needs a short pilot to tune rather than a guess upfront.
  • Who writes the outcome summary. Pure navigator entry, or AI-drafted and navigator-confirmed? This changes both the effort and the strength of the audit record.
  • Ownership SLA. For an incidental finding from an out-of-domain physician, how long before silence counts as a decline and the case falls through to a safety-net owner?
  • Reference staffing model. Which site's roles do we configure against first? Whatever we pick becomes the implicit default others are compared to.
