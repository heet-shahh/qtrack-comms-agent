Voice Agent Platform Selection

2026-07-20 Team 4 Retell · Vapi · ElevenLabs · Bland Decision doc

We evaluated the voice platforms for placing the automatic patient calls (Track A) and the warm handoff to a navigator (the fallout escalation). The recommendation is Retell as the platform, with ElevenLabs as the voice inside it. This doc gives the reasoning, the numbers, and how it drops into the POC we already built, so anyone on the team can act on it without re-running the research.

01 Verdict

For a clinical build that must handle PHI and ship fast, the choice is not close. Three requirements decide it, and Retell is cleanest on all three: a Business Associate Agreement with no friction, letting our own engine drive the conversation, and a warm transfer to a named navigator.

  • Primary: Retell. Native phone calling (SIP/PSTN), self-serve BAA on standard plans, warm transfer with full context, and a custom-LLM websocket so our state machine drives. Its voice is ElevenLabs under the hood anyway.
  • Strong second: Vapi. Pick it only if owning and swapping every component matters more than time-to-ship and included compliance. HIPAA is a fixed paid add-on and it needs a backend engineer to tune.
  • ElevenLabs: the voice, not the platform. Best-sounding, but it has no phone-first telephony (you bolt on Twilio) and no BAA below Enterprise. We get its voice for free by choosing Retell.
  • Bland: Track A overflow only. Cheapest at high volume, but the weakest compliance story, so never for anything touching a finding.
The false binary to retire
"Retell vs ElevenLabs" is not a real choice. Retell runs ElevenLabs TTS as its voice engine, so picking Retell gets us the best voice and the telephony, compliance, and transfer plumbing we would otherwise build ourselves.

02 What we're actually choosing

We already built the brain: the interpret step, the playbooks, the state machine. So we are not shopping for a "chatbot that decides what to say." We need a platform that supplies the parts we do not want to build (telephony, speech-to-text, text-to-speech, turn-taking) and then gets out of the way so our engine runs the dialogue. That reframes the whole comparison around four criteria.

A design point that shrinks the requirement
Our architecture already routes every open-clinical conversation to a human navigator. The voice agent only ever runs binary-logistics Track A calls plus the escalation handoff. So we do not need a clinical-safety-tuned model (that is Hippocratic AI's pitch), because the agent never has the risky conversation. That narrows the field to exactly what Retell does best, and defers the expensive clinical-native platforms.

03 The call path

This is what a live Track A call looks like on Retell, and why the fit is exact: the platform owns the phone and the speech, our existing brain owns the decision, and the fallout branch becomes a native warm transfer. Step through it.

→ → → → →
Step 1 · Retell places the call

Retell dials the patient over native SIP/PSTN. No Twilio account to stitch, no separate telephony vendor. This is the piece we most want to not build.

Step 2 · Speech to text

The patient speaks; Retell's built-in turn-taking and STT convert it to a transcript in real time, holding a steady round-trip so the caller does not feel the machine.

Step 3 · Our brain decides (unchanged from the POC)

Retell streams the transcript over a custom-LLM websocket to our endpoint. That endpoint is the exact interpret() plus state machine from the POC. It returns the next line and the structured outcome. The dialogue policy is ours, not the vendor's.

Step 4 · Text to speech

Our reply is spoken back through ElevenLabs voices, which Retell runs as its TTS engine. Best-in-class voice, no separate contract.

Step 5 · Matched outcome

On a clean match (a captured date, a confirmation), our backend writes the event-log row and spawns the next task, identical to the Slack path. The channel changed; the engine did not.

Step 6 · The other branch: warm transfer

On fallout or a barrier signal, our brain triggers Retell's warm transfer to the assigned navigator, carrying the captured question as context. This is our three-move handoff, delivered natively instead of as a Slack card.

05 HIPAA & compliance

This is the deciding factor for a clinical POC, and it is also where public sources disagree most, so it is worth stating precisely rather than as a single verdict.

PlatformBAA availabilityExtra costNotes
Retell Self-serve BAA, sign in the dashboard No add-on fee on standard / pay-as-you-go PII redaction is a ~$0.01/min surcharge; countersign often within a business day.
Vapi Zero-Data-Retention mode with BAA ~$1,000/mo add-on (one older source says $2,000) Flat fixed cost regardless of volume; hard to justify pre-revenue.
ElevenLabs BAA on Enterprise contract Custom enterprise pricing A $99/mo Pro user must jump to a negotiated contract for a BAA.
Bland Included in standard pricing (per Bland) No surcharge claimed But described elsewhere as not designed for telephony-compliance workflows.
The one honest conflict on Retell
Most sources (including an independent tester and Retell's own detailed BAA doc) say the self-serve BAA is available on standard / pay-as-you-go plans at no extra fee. A minority (one dev writeup, and a competitor's pricing blog) claim Retell restricts HIPAA to Enterprise. The weight of evidence favors self-serve, but this is exactly the kind of term that changes quietly, so confirm with Retell's security team in writing before we put PHI through it.

Reading the numbers
Under 800ms is the threshold where callers stop noticing they are talking to a machine. Retell's ~620ms out of the box, with no tuning, is the practical win for us; Vapi can beat it but only with a hand-tuned Deepgram plus fast-LLM plus Cartesia stack that we would own and maintain.

07 Plugging into the POC

The migration is small on purpose. The POC was built with the channel as a swappable adapter, so voice is a new adapter, not a rebuild. Here is the whole path.

→ → → →
Step 1 · Keep the brain untouched

The interpret() function, the playbooks, the state machine, and the event log stay exactly as they are. This is the whole point of the seam we built.

Step 2 · Add a VoiceAdapter

Implement the same interface as the Slack adapter (a way to start a turn and a callback for the patient's reply). It just speaks to Retell instead of Slack.

Step 3 · Point Retell at our endpoint

Configure Retell's custom-LLM websocket to call our brain endpoint. Every transcript turn now flows into the same logic that drove the text demo.

Step 4 · Route fallout to warm transfer

Where the POC posted a Slack card on escalation, the voice path calls Retell's warm-transfer API with the captured question as context. Same escalation logic, native delivery.

Step 5 · Sign the BAA, flip the flag

Sign the BAA, enable PII redaction, set short recording retention, then set the channel config to voice. Track A calls now go out by phone; nothing else in the system changes.
